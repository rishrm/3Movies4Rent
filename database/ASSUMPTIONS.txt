DEMO DATA AND DESIGN DECISIONS

1. The original team tables and columns are retained. PERSON and EmployeeType
   already correct the two modeling deductions from Project 1. The comma after
   PRIMARY KEY (OrderID) is also present in the revised schema.

2. Two nullable foreign keys were tightened: CUSTOMER.PersonID and QUEUES.MovieID
   are NOT NULL. Each customer must have a person record, and every queue entry
   must name a movie. The redundant UNIQUE(AccountNumber, QueuePosition) was
   replaced with UNIQUE(AccountNumber, MovieID), adopting a no-duplicate-title
   queue policy. Queue positions are maintained as consecutive positive integers.

3. Official demo source:
   https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~sas/courses/cse305/fall26/project/demo_data.html
   The official data omits the representatives who handled orders 1-4. For this
   test fixture, all four are assigned to David Smith, the only listed customer
   representative. This is an added assumption, not a fact supplied by the demo.
   The queue order follows the order in which movies appear in the demo account
   table. Initial RATES is empty because individual user ratings are not supplied.

4. The demo gives dates, rather than complete return timestamps. Order 1 uses
   midnight on its stated return date. Order 2 uses its rental timestamp as the
   return timestamp, keeping it on the stated return date and avoiding a return
   before rental. These are timestamp assumptions already used by the team draft.
   Historical dates remain in 2005, 2006 and 2009; they are not moved to 2026.

5. Monthly subscription income uses the assignment's fixed prices: Lim $10,
   Unlim1 $15, Unlim2 $20 and Unlim3 $25. The supplied schema records no account
   cancellation, historical plan changes, invoices or payments. The report is
   therefore labeled an estimate assuming every retained account stays active
   after creation, its current plan also applied in the requested month, and a
   full fee applies without proration. Exact historical accounting requires a
   schema extension and corresponding historical data. No such history is invented.

6. Rentals use calendar months for the limited plan's two-rental monthly quota.
   Every successful rental counts, including one returned on the same day. Open
   rentals determine simultaneous-use limits and copy availability. The account
   cannot rent a second concurrent copy of a movie it already holds. Returning a
   movie preserves its order and all links and offers the next eligible, available
   queued title. Unavailable titles are skipped; if none is eligible, it waits.

7. Deletion is allowed for unreferenced customer, employee and movie records.
   Historical rental references prevent deletion. This avoids destroying history
   or leaving an unreturned movie disconnected from its customer. A PERSON row
   is kept if the person still has another customer/employee subtype.

8. Recommendations use distinct previously rented titles as customer history.
   Similarity is the number of shared titles. All customers tied for the highest
   positive overlap are nearest neighbors. Their unseen titles are ranked by the
   number of recommending neighbors, then MovieID. The same query serves customer
   representatives and customers. A new customer without overlapping history gets
   an empty recommendation list; best sellers remain a separate list.

9. Individual movie ratings are in RATES. A customer can rate only a movie rented
   through that account; a new rating for the same pair replaces the old rating.
   MOVIE.Rating and CUSTOMER.Rating retain the supplied popularity/activity scores;
   the specification gives no formula to recompute those scores automatically.

10. Reports for rental activity and best sellers are all-time. Tied leaders are
    included. Multi-keyword and multi-actor searches mean ALL supplied values.
    Actor IDs identify actors because actor names need not be unique.

11. Procedures require an explicit READ COMMITTED transaction. Successful
    application operations COMMIT; any error ROLLBACKs. Tests ROLLBACK successful
    mutations as well, preserving the initial fixture. All rentals, returns,
    inventory edits and queue writes must use the documented transaction paths.
    On MySQL deadlock error 1213 or lock timeout 1205, retry the entire operation
    with a bounded retry policy; never continue a partially failed transaction.

12. Assignment 2 documents the DML used by each role. Login authentication and
    application authorization are not implemented by adding an EmployeeType CHECK.
    The JDBC application must authorize manager/representative operations and bind
    customer identity from the authenticated session, not from a user-editable
    customer-ID field. Customer queries check account ownership; the representative
    employee query omits HourlyRate. Do not give customers direct table access.
    The test runner's database credentials are for testing, not customer logins.
